Scholarship Student Services Awareness Campaign — Graduation Project

An independently planned and designed graduation campaign introducing international scholarship students to university services and opportunities. Developed from January to June 2024 and approved by the Scholarship Office at King Abdulaziz University, the campaign was not implemented.

pr media campaign approved not implemented Inkscape, Renderforest, Adobe Express, Canva
Project details

Brief

This graduation project proposed an awareness campaign about the services, programmes, and opportunities available to international scholarship students at King Abdulaziz University.

The campaign aimed to increase awareness of scholarship student support, encourage communication and participation, and strengthen students’ sense of belonging. Its materials introduced the Scholarship Administration and highlighted services such as visas and travel tickets, alongside student programmes and activities.

Developed between January and June 2024, the project combined campaign planning, Arabic content, graphic design, and video elements into a coordinated communication package.

My role

I independently planned and designed the entire project. My responsibilities covered the campaign concept, objectives, content planning, Arabic copy, logo design, visual direction, poster production, and preparation of the video introduction and outro.

I developed the materials from the initial idea through to the completed campaign package, which received approval from the Scholarship Office.

Process

I organised the campaign around the information needs of international scholarship students. General campaign materials introduced its purpose and encouraged participation, while an institutional overview presented the administration’s services, programmes, and activities. Dedicated posters explained individual services, including visas and travel tickets.

I used recurring colours, geometric elements, institutional imagery, and consistent visual details to connect the materials. Each poster had a specific communication purpose within the wider campaign, helping move the audience from a general introduction to more focused service information.

I designed the project’s logo in Inkscape, created the video introduction and outro using Renderforest, and used Adobe Express and Canva for the remaining designs.

Outcome and lessons

The project resulted in a coordinated campaign package that received approval from the Scholarship Office. It was not implemented, so its outcomes relate to the completed planning and creative materials rather than measured audience reach, participation, or awareness.

Planning and designing the project independently gave me practical experience in connecting communication objectives with visual outputs. It strengthened my ability to organise Arabic information, maintain consistency across multiple materials, and select tools for different production tasks.

The project also highlighted the value of concise copy, clear visual hierarchy, and an evaluation plan for assessing campaign effectiveness if implemented.

Tools used

Inkscape — logo design.

Renderforest — video introduction and outro.

Adobe Express and Canva — campaign graphics and remaining designs.

Project information

Period: January–June 2024.

Project type: Academic graduation project.

Institutional context: King Abdulaziz University.

Status: Approved by the Scholarship Office; not implemented.

Access for All — Make Learning Accessible

A fictional educational campaign combining practical accessibility guidance, social media content, and video ads to encourage clearer, more inclusive learning materials.

pr media campaign concept Canva — graphic design, content layouts, video assembly, animation, and exports.
Project details

Brief

Develop a campaign encouraging tutors, education coordinators, student groups, and small training providers to review their learning materials and make one practical improvement. The campaign focused on clearer layouts, meaningful image descriptions, and accurate captions, supported by an accessibility checklist.

My role

I handled campaign planning, message development, copywriting, graphic design, storyboarding, and video production. I also adapted the creative direction across campaign content and advertising formats.

Process

I built the campaign around a manageable action: “One resource. One improvement.” This gave the audience a clear starting point. Before-and-after examples demonstrated how small changes could improve the clarity of teaching materials.

I used a consistent palette of navy, off-white, pale teal, and yellow, with Open Sans typography. The content moved from awareness to practical guidance, directing viewers towards the checklist. The campaign included graphics, a carousel, Stories, and video, alongside a main video ad, a short cutdown, and two static ads.

Outcome and lessons

I completed a coordinated campaign and advertising sample supported by a practical checklist. The project strengthened my ability to explain accessibility through clear examples and turn an educational message into a focused call to action.

It also taught me to distinguish between demonstrating useful improvements and claiming that a resource is fully accessible. As a fictional portfolio project, the outcome reflects the completed creative work rather than measured audience impact.

Bridge Arabic — Start with a Word

A bilingual campaign and advertising concept designed to make beginner Arabic learning feel welcoming, practical, and achievable.

pr media campaign concept Canva — bilingual layouts, campaign graphics, and advertising assets.
Project details

Brief

Create a promotional campaign for a fictional Arabic-learning offer aimed at beginners who speak other languages. The campaign needed to introduce the offer clearly, reduce hesitation about starting, and encourage potential learners to explore it through approachable messages and supporting visuals.

My role

I developed the campaign direction, audience messaging, bilingual copy, visual content, and advertising concepts. I brought together my background in Arabic and communication to shape an accessible introduction to the learning offer.

Process

I centred the campaign on “Start with a word,” presenting a small first step that a beginner could imagine taking. The messaging connected learning Arabic with everyday communication and gradual progress.

I developed Arabic and English content with attention to reading direction, visual hierarchy, and consistency. Across the campaign and ad materials, I kept the message focused on the learner’s starting point, using concise copy and clear calls to action.

Outcome and lessons

The project produced a bilingual campaign and advertising portfolio sample with a consistent message across its creative materials. It demonstrates my ability to connect audience needs, language knowledge, copywriting, and visual communication.

I learned that bilingual design requires more than translating words: both languages need space, readable layouts, and an appropriate tone. The project also reinforced the value of encouraging realistic first steps without promising instant fluency.

Calligraphy — Make Your First Mark

A campaign and advertising concept for a fictional beginner Arabic calligraphy workshop, built around the confidence to begin with one stroke.

pr media campaign concept Canva — campaign layouts, graphics, video editing, animation, and vertical video exports.
Project details

Brief

Develop a campaign introducing Arabic calligraphy to beginners who are interested in the art but unsure where to start. The workshop concept focused on guided practice with basic strokes and letterforms. The communication needed to make the first step feel approachable while respecting the practice involved in developing the skill.

My role

I handled campaign planning, copywriting, visual direction, graphic design, storyboarding, and video production. I developed the campaign and advertising materials around a shared beginner-focused message.

Process

I used “Make Your First Mark” as the central campaign message. The campaign video, “Every practice page starts with one mark,” introduced progress through practice. The main ad addressed a different moment: the beginner’s reaction, “That looks complicated. Where do I start?”

The selected advertising concept, “Start Small,” answered that hesitation with “Begin with one stroke.” I carried this progression into an eight-second cutdown and used the call to action “Explore the sample workshop details.” The videos used concise on-screen text to communicate without voiceover, with clear fictional-workshop disclosure.

Outcome and lessons

I completed a coordinated workshop campaign and advertising sample, including social content, a campaign video, a main ad, a short cutdown, and static advertising materials.

The project strengthened my ability to turn a perceived barrier into a creative message. I learned how pacing, short text, and a simple visual progression can make an unfamiliar activity feel approachable without overstating what a beginner can achieve.

Evergreen Catalog — Find a Useful Starting Point

A campaign and advertising concept promoting a demonstration catalog of communication kits for solo creators and small teams.

pr media campaign concept Canva — campaign graphics, social layouts, video production, and advertising exports.
Project details

Brief

Create a campaign introducing Evergreen Catalog as a place to explore sample communication resources. The audience included solo creators and people managing communication for small teams. The campaign needed to explain the range clearly, while the advertising focused on a specific resource: the 30-Day Social Calendar sample.

My role

I handled campaign planning, message development, copywriting, graphic design, storyboarding, video production, and quality checks. I adapted the campaign direction into social content and several advertising formats.

Process

I built the broader campaign around “Find a useful starting point,” supported by the call to action “Browse the kits.” This positioned the catalog around a practical audience need.

For the main ad, I selected the concept “What will you post next?” and focused on the 30-Day Social Calendar sample. This gave the advertising a specific problem and resource to introduce. Supporting content showed the wider range, including podcast, crisis-statement, and brand-voice resources.

I developed graphics, a carousel, Stories, and a campaign video, followed by an approximately 26-second main ad, an eight-second cutdown, and two static ads. Final checks covered mobile readability, message consistency, video presentation, and accurate sample disclosure.

Outcome and lessons

I completed a campaign and advertising package that connects a broad catalog offer with a focused promotional message. The project demonstrates my ability to organise related assets while giving each format a clear purpose.

I learned that a catalog becomes easier to communicate when the main ad highlights one recognisable audience problem. The project also strengthened my ability to maintain consistency between awareness content, resource promotion, and calls to action. Outcomes relate to the completed portfolio materials; no sales or engagement results are claimed.
